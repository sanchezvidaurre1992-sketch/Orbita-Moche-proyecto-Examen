Water resilience and smart crop rotation from space

On the northern coast of Peru, severe water scarcity at the tail-end of the Tinajones Reservoir system—most critically in Mórrope and San José, alongside valley districts like Túcume, Mochumí, and Ferreñafe—forces smallholders and local workers to resort to improvised open-pit or tubular wells. While large-scale agro-export corporations extract deep groundwater unchecked, small farmers in tail-end zones face the severe daily threat of toxic brackish water, irreversible soil salinization, and critical public health risks. Órbita Moche translates open NASA satellite data into actionable early irrigation alerts and sustainable soil protection.
